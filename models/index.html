Models

Three models describe how the Wasatch Front region lives, works and travels.

WFRC and its partners maintain three planning models. They are developed in the open, and the pages below explain what each one does and where to find its documentation.

Travel Demand Model

Forecasts how people travel on the region’s roads and transit. Built with Mountainland Association of Governments (MAG).

Current release 9.2.0

Learn more

Real-Estate Market Model

Simulates where households and jobs may locate, from available land and market conditions, on the UrbanSim platform.

Current release 3.0.0

Learn more

Bike Demand Model

Estimates bicycle trips between small zones called microzones and assigns them to the bike network.

Learn more

TipWant to use a model?

Access is by request. The Get the model guide explains who to ask, what to read first and where to download.

How the models fit together

The Real-Estate Market Model’s household and job projections drive trip generation in the Travel Demand Model. In turn, the Real-Estate Market Model uses travel accessibility from the Travel Demand Model when it predicts where development happens. The Bike Demand Model works at a finer scale, with its own zones and network.

Coming next: an activity-based model

WFRC began an implementation project for an Activity-Based Model (ABM) in March 2026, expected to take about three years. It is not available yet. See the WFRC models page for updates.
